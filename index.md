## 1. Welcome to Trekbok
Trekbok is a dedicated travel day counter and trip logger built for iOS. It is designed to take the guesswork out of tracking your time abroad—whether you are monitoring strict visa allowances (such as the Schengen 90/180-day rule), tracking days for tax residency thresholds, or simply keeping a clear, chronological log of your journeys.

Instead of relying on messy spreadsheets or complex multi-step tools, Trekbok focuses on speed, clarity, and precision: open the app, log your dates, and instantly see where you stand.

### Core Philosophy
* **100% Offline by Design:** Travel often means patchy Wi-Fi, airplane mode, or no roaming data. Trekbok works entirely offline. All calculations, trip logs, and summaries function without an active internet connection.
* **On-Device & Private:** Your travel history is personal. Trekbok requires no account creation, no sign-ups, and no passwords. All trip details and calculations remain securely on your iPhone.
* **No Subscriptions:** Essential features are free forever. Advanced tools are unlocked via a transparent, single one-time purchase.
* **Fast & Native:** Built natively for iOS, Trekbok starts up instantly and integrates seamlessly with your device without unnecessary battery drain or clutter.

## 2. Getting Started: The Basics

### First-Time Setup
When you first launch Trekbok, you will be prompted to enter the country you are currently in and the date you arrived. 

If you do not remember the exact arrival date, pick your best estimate—you can easily adjust it later. This entry establishes your active trip, and Trekbok will immediately begin counting your days.

### Logging Trips
Manage your trips directly from the **History** tab. There are two ways to add a journey:

* **Cross a Border (Active Trips):** Use this when transitioning from one country to another in real time. You will confirm your departure date from your previous country, select your new destination, and enter your arrival date.
* **Log a Past Trip (Completed Journeys):** Use this to backfill historical travel. Enter the destination country along with your entry and exit dates. *(Note: Only completed trips with departure dates in the past can be logged here. If a past trip conflicts with your active trip dates, Trekbok will prompt you to resolve the overlap).*

All logged journeys appear chronologically in the **History** view, and daily presence can be inspected at a glance in the **Calendar** view.

## 3. Tracking Limits & Windows

### Setting Up Trackers
From the **Home** tab, add any country you need to monitor, then tap **Add Tracker** to set its parameters:
* **Calendar / Fiscal Year:** Track days within a fixed 12-month window starting on any custom date (e.g., standard January 1st calendar year or April 6th for UK tax residency).
* **Rolling Window:** Track days across dynamic time frames, such as the standard Schengen 90 days within any 180-day period.

Once configured, an intuitive progress bar displays:
* Total days spent in the country.
* Remaining days before reaching your set limit.

### Counting Partial Days
Different immigration authorities and tax jurisdictions handle arrival and departure days differently:
* **Midnight Rule:** Only days where you were physically present at midnight count toward your total.
* **Any Presence Rule:** Any calendar day where you spent time in the country counts as a full day.

You can customize this calculation for each tracker by toggling the **Count Partial Days** switch to match your jurisdiction's rules.
